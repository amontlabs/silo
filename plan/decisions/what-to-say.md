# What to say

## The choice

An agent's part, 22 November 2026. Someone at a meetup asks what Silo is, and you have five
sentences.

The options:

- What you get (recommended). A computer of their own on your Mac, full access inside, what they
  did, a way back, any agent program. It costs the why, which is left out, and "on your Mac" says
  nothing of servers.
- What holds people back. "Three things cap what a person gets from agents: what they dare let
  agents do, how fast they can judge the result, and whether they can go back." It costs a listener
  who has nothing to picture.
- What others lack. Other products checkpoint and fork machines for agents, and Silo adds the
  record. It costs being filed next to those products before anything else is heard.

Recommended: what you get. polarzero asked for accurate, simple words, and five things a person can
picture are that. The answer is the same five lines as before, written as full sentences.

## Two lines that need their limit

An agent's part, 24 November 2026.

"It works under any agent program." Someone runs Cursor's agent in main and sets a budget. The
computer, its checkpoints and the record of processes and files work for any agent program. Reading
model calls, and so spend and budgets, works only for programs that call the model provider from
inside the computer: 8 of the 11 tried in #367 and #369. Cursor, Amp, Kiro and Windsurf call
through their vendor's servers, which the boundary cannot read. Proposed: "Checkpoints and the
record work under any agent program. What it spent, for programs that call the model provider
from the computer."

"You see what they did: what ran, what changed, where it connected and what it cost." Each part
arrives with a different build in #348: what changed with the first, what ran and where it
connected with the second, what it cost with the third. Until then, say only the parts that are
built.

## Notes

These five lines carry no mark in the design record.

polarzero's instruction, for all of it: phrase the product in accurate, simple words backed by the
research, and treat polarzero's own rough wording with caution. If interest comes from an accurate
and simple description, that is the best proof the product is interesting.

Proposed, adapted from the notes: "Three things cap what a person gets from agents: what they dare
let agents do, how fast they can judge the result, and whether they can go back. The boundary
answers the first, the record the second, checkpoints the third."

Proposed: "Several cloud products checkpoint and fork virtual machines for agents, so "computers
for agents" does not set Silo apart. The record does, together with going back and macOS
computers on your own Mac." The market was checked lightly on 16 November 2026, not surveyed.

An agent's part, 24 November 2026. A survey of about 70 products changes the last part of that
sentence. Local apps now give agents macOS virtual machines with checkpoints, so neither sets Silo
apart. Proposed in its place: "The record does, together with budgets enforced at the boundary and
checkpoints taken on their own."

"Approval" here is the approval prompts of agent programs, not anything of Silo's.

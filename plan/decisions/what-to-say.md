# What to say

## For the owner

An agent's part, 22 November 2026. Someone at a meetup asks what Silo is, and you have five
sentences.

The options:

- What you get (recommended). A computer of their own on your Mac, full access inside, what they
  did, a way back, any agent program. It costs the why, which is left out, and "on your Mac" says
  nothing of servers.
- What holds people back. "Three things cap what a person gets from agents: what they dare let
  agents do, how fast they can judge the result, and whether they can go back." It costs a listener
  who has nothing to picture.
- What others lack. Other products checkpoint and fork machines for agents, and Silo adds the
  record. It costs being filed next to those products before anything else is heard.

Recommended: what you get. You asked for accurate, simple words, and five things a person can
picture are that. The answer is the same five lines as before, written as full sentences.

These five lines carry no mark in the design record.

The owner's instruction, for all of it: phrase the product in accurate, simple words backed by the
research, and treat the owner's own rough wording with caution. If interest comes from an accurate
and simple description, that is the best proof the product is interesting.

Proposed, adapted from the notes: "Three things cap what a person gets from agents: what they dare
let agents do, how fast they can judge the result, and whether they can go back. The boundary
answers the first, the record the second, checkpoints the third."

Proposed: "Several cloud products checkpoint and fork virtual machines for agents, so "computers
for agents" does not set Silo apart. The record does, together with going back and macOS
computers on your own Mac." The market was checked lightly on 16 November 2026, not surveyed.

"Approval" here is the approval prompts of agent programs, not anything of Silo's.

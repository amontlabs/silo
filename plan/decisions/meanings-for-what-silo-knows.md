# The meanings of the words for what Silo knows

## For the owner

An agent's part, 22 November 2026. On Tuesday an agent in main installs a package. On Friday a
classifier you added says the package was not needed. The install is in main's record. The
classifier's view is an opinion, kept beside it.

The options:

- The fuller meaning (recommended). "Everything Silo has written down about a computer: its facts
  and its reports, in the order they came, never changed afterwards. Engineers call this an
  append-only log. Opinions are about it and are kept apart." It is read as one list for each
  computer, in order, that nothing edits. It costs a longer meaning, and it commits Silo to the
  order and to never changing a line.
- The meaning in force. "Facts and reports are written once, and together they are the record." It
  is read as a name for the facts and reports taken together. It costs three answers the word then
  does not give: whose record it is, whether it has an order, and where opinions go.

Recommended: the fuller meaning. The record is the core of the product, and people rely on exactly
these three things when they read it.

The record's word list (section 2) carries no mark. A word's meaning is in force only where a
line marked Decided or Agreed, or settled in STATE, holds it. What this decision sets is the
word list's own meaning, which replaces the word in `words.json` when taken.

- record: in force as "Facts and reports are written once, and together they are the record." (record l.502).
- classifier: in force as "Whatever gives an opinion." Its new meaning is put forward by
  `what-makes-a-classifier` alone, with "labeler" in its `not`.

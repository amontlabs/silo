# Who else Silo is for

## The choice

An agent's part, 27 November 2026. polarzero asked that Silo be put "in perspective with models
alignment, tracking, experiments". Someone who studies agents wants to know how often an agent's
own summary disagrees with what it did, per model, on real tasks. Today they build a harness for
it. In Silo the record already holds both sides, a fork runs the same task again from the same
checkpoint, and a classifier can be tried on the recorded past (`extension-trial-on-the-past`).

The options:

- Developers and teams first, then people who study agents (recommended). The product stays the
  one polarzero uses. The second audience gets no feature of its own, only what the first already
  needs. It costs a promise to people who mostly work on Linux servers and run many computers at
  once, which Silo does later (`where-it-runs`).
- Developers and teams only. It costs nothing to build. It leaves out the people for whom the
  record against the agent's own account is the whole point.
- People who study agents first. It costs the first user, who is a developer on a Mac
  (`who-it-is-for`).

Recommended: developers and teams first, then people who study agents.

## The earlier choice, 22 November 2026

An agent's part, 22 November 2026. A developer keeps one agent open in a terminal and reads each
step. A team of five keeps twenty agents going on different tasks. Both could use Silo. The question
is whom the site and the next features speak to after you.

The options:

- Developers and teams who run many agents and move fast (recommended). The site speaks to the team
  of five and to people who work like you. It costs the careful developer with one agent, whose
  wishes rank lower.
- Any developer who uses an agent. The audience is as wide as it gets. It costs a vague pitch, and
  it draws people who want approval prompts and safety first, which Silo does not lead with.

Recommended: developers and teams who run many agents and move fast. polarzero said it, and the record and
going back matter most to people who cannot read every step.

# The meanings of the words for a computer

## For the owner

An agent's part, 22 November 2026. Main runs three agents. You pause it for lunch and resume it: the
three are where they were. Later one agent loops, and you stop that one. At night you stop main: all
three end, and the disk stays.

The options:

- Stop covers a run and a computer (recommended). "End running programs: one run, or every program
  in a computer. The disk stays." It is read as the stop button people know, on an agent or on a
  computer. It costs saying each time which of the two is stopped.
- Stop is for a computer only. "End the programs running in a computer. Its disk stays." It is read
  as shutting the computer down. It costs a second word for ending one agent's run, and the feature
  `stopping-a-run` already says "stop" for it.

Recommended: stop covers both. It is one idea, ending programs that no checkpoint keeps, and a
second word for it would be one more to learn. The nine other words are read as written either way:
a pause freezes a computer and loses nothing, a drop sends a fork or a fresh computer to the bin,
and a checkpoint is a saved moment that never changes.

## Notes

The record's word list (section 2) carries no mark. A word's meaning is in force only where a
line marked Decided or Agreed, or settled in STATE, holds it. What this decision sets is the
word list's own meaning, which replaces the word in `words.json` when taken.

- computer: no marked line holds its meaning.
- fork: no marked line holds its meaning.
- fresh computer: no marked line holds its meaning.
- checkpoint: no marked line holds its meaning.
- restore: no marked line holds its meaning.
- drop: no marked line holds its meaning.
- bin: in force as "Where a dropped computer waits." (STATE: a dropped computer waits in a bin).
- pause: no marked line holds its meaning.
- stop: no marked line holds its meaning.
- only here: no marked line can be cut into its meaning (record l.1168, l.1174), so the word is
  not in force.

# The order of work

## The choice

An agent's part, 22 November 2026. Two things could be built next: the record of what happened in
main, or forks you can show in a demo next week.

The options:

- The record first (recommended). Main on macOS and its record come first, then forks, with recipes
  early because a Linux computer beside a macOS main needs one. It costs the demo: what is easiest
  to show arrives after something less visible.
- What shows best first. Forks and going back lead. It costs the centre of Silo: without the record,
  what is built is the part other products already have, by the light check of 16 November.

Recommended: the record first. It is what you fixed line by line in the discussion, and it puts
first what sets Silo apart.

## Notes

The answer is worded by whoever wrote the plan. What the discussion said about order, with the
design record's marks:

- The order of work is polarzero's.
- No time estimates. (Decided)
- The record is the core. Merging is not.
- Main on macOS, and forks on macOS or Linux, have priority. (Owner) A fork is a copy, so a Linux
  computer beside a macOS main is a fresh computer, not a copy.
- Recipes and tasks are wanted, and with a macOS main they are needed early, because a Linux
  computer can only be made from a recipe.
- Giving memory back when idle, and forks that share their parent's disk, move early. (Agreed)
- The hot-file map comes once the record exists, since it costs little then. (Agreed)
- Extensions follow the record. The first piece is a program that forwards
  alerts and asks.
- Recording the screen when an agent drives it is useful and not first. (Owner)
- The phone app is later. (Owner)
- Speed is part of the guidelines from the start. (Owner)

Lines with no mark are what the discussion fixed, as the design record gives them. "The first
piece" is the record's wording; forwarding alerts and asks to a phone is now the later feature
`forwarding-to-a-phone`.

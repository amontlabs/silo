# Why stop, restore and drop are destructive

## The choice

An agent's part, 22 November 2026. You stop a fork in which an agent has a dev server running and a
build halfway through.

The options:

- What they end (recommended). Stop, restore and drop are destructive because the programs running
  in the computer, and what they hold in memory, are lost. The disk is kept: a stop leaves it, a
  drop keeps it in the bin, and a restore first saves where you were. A restore you choose to make
  without saving also loses what is on the disk and in no checkpoint. It costs a filing that can
  change: if checkpoints one day keep memory, the three are asked again, and with them who may
  trigger them.
- Destructive by name, for good. The three stay destructive whatever checkpoints come to keep. It
  costs the meaning every other action is filed by, since these three would stop following it once
  nothing is lost.

Recommended: what they end. It is what the three really lose today, and it keeps every action filed
by the same meaning.

## Notes

That they are destructive is in force (`kinds-of-action`). Why is not decided: this is the
reason put forward.

# The meanings of the words for what Silo does

## The choice

An agent's part, 22 November 2026. The rule that alerts you when main reaches a new host ships with
Silo, switched on. You never set it. The meaning put forward for "rule" begins "Data you set".

The options:

- Only "alert" (recommended). "Rule" and "precaution" keep the one sentence each that you already
  took, and "alert" gets a meaning for the first time. It costs nothing you have today: that rules
  are data and that a rule allows, holds or denies are said in their own decisions.
- All three as written. "Rule" and "precaution" gain "Data you set", and "rule" gains "At the
  boundary it allows, holds or denies". It costs accuracy, since a rule that ships with Silo or
  comes with an extension is not one you set, and the same two things are then said in two places.

Recommended: only "alert". A word's meaning should be the one sentence that is true of every case,
and the two in force already are.

## Notes

The record's word list (section 2) carries no mark. A word's meaning is in force only where a
line marked Decided or Agreed, or settled in STATE, holds it. What this decision sets is the
word list's own meaning, which replaces the word in `words.json` when taken.

- rule: in force as the marked line of 5.4: "A rule is triggered by a fact. It is exact, and it may do anything you wrote in it.".
- precaution: in force as the marked line of 5.4, with the settled word "protective": "A precaution is triggered by a report or an opinion. It can be wrong, so it takes only protective actions.".
- protective: in force as the marked meaning of the table of kinds (5.4): "Said of an action that grants nothing and destroys nothing. A wrong one costs only time.". The marked line holds its whole meaning, so this decision does not set it.
- alert: no marked line holds its meaning.

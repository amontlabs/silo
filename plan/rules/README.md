# Rules and precautions

What Silo does by itself: a rule on a fact, a precaution on a report or an opinion.

## For

To let Silo act on what it knows while you are away, without letting something that can be
wrong grant anything or destroy anything.

## Why it is so

- **Two ways to act, told apart by their trigger** (`rule`, `precaution`). A wrong grant lets
  something out that cannot be taken back, and a wrong pause only costs time. So what may be
  undone may be done on a doubt, and what cannot needs a fact or you (`less-sure-less-power`,
  `law-of-triggers`, `grant-needs-a-fact-or-you`).
- **Every action is filed under a kind before it is designed** (`kind-before-design`,
  `kinds-of-action`), because the kind decides who may trigger it. "To grant" is the term access
  control uses for giving the ability to do something; its opposite is "to revoke".
- **A precaution is the only way an opinion acts** (`how-an-opinion-acts`,
  `what-a-precaution-may-do`), and it is kept outside the computer (`where-precautions-live`).
- **Rules have no order** (`which-rule-wins`): which one wins never depends on when it was
  written (`rule-order-never-matters`).

## One example

A rule allows crates.io, and another alerts on any new host. A program in the computer connects
to paste.rs. No rule allows it, so it is denied, and you are alerted.
